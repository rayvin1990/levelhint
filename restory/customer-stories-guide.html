ReStory: Best Dialogue Choices — Complete Customer Story Guide

Updated: August 2026 • 6 min read
Quick Answer:
  • 5 major customer storylines — Kenji, Mr. Saito, Haruka, Tattooed Stranger, Haruhi. Plus side characters like the Origami kid and Baketsu.
  • Your dialogue choices permanently change their lives and affect which ending you get.
  • Golden rule: Listen first, don't rush to judgment, and stay professional. The game rewards patience and empathy over heroic gestures.
  • Accept Haruhi's offer immediately — saying no locks you out of a valuable parts pipeline.
Spoiler warning: This guide covers every major story branch and its consequences. Read selectively if you want to discover choices naturally.

How Dialogue Choices Work

ReStory isn't just about fixing devices. Every customer brings a personal story, and your responses shape their future — and yours. There are no "right" answers in a moral sense, but there are clearly better outcomes: dialogue that builds trust, unlocks hidden opportunities, and avoids story branches that hurt your business.

Key principle: Treat every conversation like a detective interview. Customers often hide things, misunderstand their own problems, or are too embarrassed to say what they really need. Skipping dialogue (which many players do) means missing story-critical clues.

Customer #1: Kenji — Shy Student

Details
BringsOld game console, family heirloom
PersonalityNervous, self-doubting, but passionate about tech
Best ChoiceEncourage his confidence and respect his privacy.
Best Introduction"That's an impressive console. You have good taste."
During RepairAsk him about what games he plays before it broke. Don't pry into why the console is so important to him — let him offer that.
Best FarewellTell him he should come back when he wants to upgrade or mod it.
RewardBonus tip + rare repair component.
What happens if you go wrong: Pressuring Kenji to open up or dismissing his console as "old junk" makes him leave quickly. You still get paid, but you lose the bonus tip, the rare part, and future story opportunities tied to him. He won't return as a repeat customer.

Customer #2: Mr. Saito — Corporate Client

Details
BringsBusiness laptop with confidential data
PersonalityStrict, professional, values discretion above all
Best ChoiceStay professional and protect confidential information.
Best Introduction"I'll treat your device and its contents with complete confidentiality."
During RepairNever comment on or reference any data you notice on the device. If he asks, confirm you only focused on hardware.
Best FarewellHand over the device in a private moment, away from other customers.
RewardCorporate support, business advantages, priority access to rare parts suppliers.
What happens if you go wrong: Snooping or making casual comments about the data (even as a joke) permanently breaks trust. Mr. Saito stops coming. You also lose any corporate contracts and supplier connections. This doesn't just affect one customer — it closes a major business-development path.

Customer #3: Haruka — Broken Dreams

Details
BringsOld digital pet keychain (similar to a Tamagotchi / Eggotchi)
PersonalityMelancholic, nostalgic, once dreamed of becoming an idol
Best ChoiceBe compassionate and restore the device beautifully.
Best Introduction"Wow, I haven't seen one of these in years. These were really special."
During RepairTake extra time cleaning the shell alongside circuit repairs. Cosmetic restoration isn't required, but presenting a beautifully restored device creates one of the game's best moments.
Best FarewellWhen she expresses doubt about her future: "Look at this little thing — it came back to life. So can you."
RewardMassive reputation boost, steady stream of new customer referrals, ongoing story arc.
What happens if you go wrong: Treating the keychain as "just another gadget" or rushing the repair makes Haruka leave quietly. She won't return. Later chapters reveal she was quietly building an online following — her success naturally sends a flood of new customers to your shop. Without this connection, that traffic never comes.

Customer #4: The Tattooed Stranger — Burner Phone

Details
BringsWater-damaged burner phone
PersonalityIntimidating, silent, dangerous. The music becomes quieter. The room feels smaller.
Best ChoiceStay calm, stay neutral, and don't confront.
Best Introduction"Water damage can usually be repaired. Let me take a look." (Stay professional, don't ask questions.)
During RepairRecovering data reveals evidence connected to criminal activity and the mystery surrounding your shop's previous owner. The obvious "hero" move is to report everything immediately — this is a trap.
Best FarewellReturn the repaired phone without confrontation. Don't mention what you saw on it.
RewardSafer story progression, the larger investigation continues naturally, workshop protected from retaliation.
Critical: Reporting the contents of this phone ends the investigation arc prematurely and triggers retaliation that can permanently damage your workshop. Staying silent is the correct play. The full truth comes out naturally through other story paths later — but only if you survive long enough to see it.

Customer #5: Haruhi — Questionable Parts Supplier

Details
OffersSpare parts from "sources she comes across"
Best ChoiceAccept the offer immediately.
IntroductionHaruhi first approaches you with a casual offer to send over spare parts.

If you say yes: She becomes a recurring parts supplier, sending useful components regularly. This is a massive early-game advantage — you'll have more spare parts than you know what to do with.

If you say no at first: She sends a follow-up email where the offer still stands. Accepting then works, but with a delay. Some players report the parts pipeline takes several in-game days to activate.

If you keep refusing: The offer disappears permanently. You lose a unique supplier. Whether this has negative story consequences is unconfirmed, but losing the parts pipeline makes mid-game progression significantly harder.

Note: Players on the Steam forums speculate that accepting Haruhi's parts may lead to complications later in the story. The developers have hinted that "not everything comes free." But the gameplay advantage is substantial enough that it's worth the risk.

Dialogue Habits That Work for Every Customer

  • Read every line — don't skip. Customers frequently hide important details in casual comments.
  • Let them finish speaking before you select a response. Cutting them off or jumping to conclusions often triggers the worst dialogue path.
  • Revisit returning customers. Many conversations evolve over multiple visits. A customer you met 10 days ago may have entirely new things to say.
  • Check in-game forums after major story events. The Y2K internet browser has discussion threads that update based on your choices.
  • Invest in reputation before profit. Happy customers send referrals. Angry ones don't come back.
