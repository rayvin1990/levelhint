ReStory: Chill Electronics Repairs — How to Make Money Fast (5 Best Yen Strategies)

Updated: August 2026 • 5 min read
Quick Answer:
  • Priority #1: Salvage components from abandoned devices instead of buying new parts. This is the single biggest profit lever.
  • Priority #2: Buy the electric screwdriver and multimeter upgrades before anything else. Faster diagnostics = more tickets per day.
  • Priority #3: Prioritize console and audio receiver repair tickets over smartphone screens (triple the profit per hour).
  • Priority #4: Combine all part orders into one shipment. Don't order per-repair — the shipping fees kill your margins.
  • Priority #5: Expand workbench capacity before buying decorations. Parallel repair jobs double your daily output.

1. Salvage Before You Buy — The #1 Profit Lever

The parts catalog is the single biggest money drain in ReStory. New players order fresh components for every repair and watch their yen disappear. The fix: strip abandoned devices for parts before buying anything.

When a customer leaves a device unfixed (or brings in a junk trade-in), don't recycle it immediately. Open it up, extract every functional capacitor, microchip, and connector. A broken boombox often has 3-4 perfectly good components that cost 500-1,200¥ each from the catalog. Reusing those salvaged parts on a console repair ticket means you pocket the full customer budget — zero overhead.

Rule of thumb: If a component on a salvage device tests green during diagnostics, extract and store it. Your repair margins will jump 30-50% within the first week.

2. Upgrade Tools Before Spending on Decorations

The game gives you a shop decoration system early. Ignore it. Your first 3,000¥ should go to:

  1. Electric screwdriver (1,200¥) — Cuts disassembly time by roughly half on devices with 8+ screws. Most consoles and audio receivers.
  2. Better multimeter / diagnostic equipment (1,500¥) — Cuts fault-finding by 40%. The bottleneck isn't your repair speed, it's finding what's broken.
  3. Improved soldering station (varies) — Needed for console motherboard repairs, which are the highest-paying tickets.

Decorations and furniture are cosmetics until your daily revenue is stable. Prioritize tools that make you faster, not items that make the shop look nicer.

4. Combine Orders — Shipping is a Silent Profit Killer

The in-game parts marketplace looks convenient. Order a capacitor here, a connector there. But each shipment costs 100-300¥ in fees. If you order parts for 3 repairs separately, you're losing 600-900¥ in shipping alone.

Instead: diagnose 3-4 devices first, then order all needed parts in one shipment. End each workday by placing tomorrow's combined order before leaving the shop. Deliveries arrive faster overnight.

5. Expand Workbench Capacity for Parallel Repairs

The workbench size limits how many devices you can work on simultaneously. With the default 1-slot bench, you repair one device at a time — diagnose, fix, deliver, then start the next. With a 2-slot bench (or 3-slot), you can:

  • Leave a console motherboard cooling under the soldering station while running diagnostics on an audio receiver
  • Start the next repair during another device's 5-10 minute wait timer (cleaning, glue drying)
  • Process 8-12 tickets per day instead of 4-5

Workbench upgrades cost more upfront than tools, but the throughput gain is permanent. If you're clearing 4 tickets per day at 1,000¥ each (4,000¥ total), upgrading to 2-slot bench pushes you to ~6-7 tickets = 6,000-7,000¥.

Common Money Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Buying the same screw twice. Track screws during disassembly — the game shows a screw counter. Missing one forces a full re-disassembly.
  2. Selling broken devices immediately. Always strip for parts first, then sell the empty shell.
  3. Accepting every ticket blindly. A full queue of 200¥ phone repairs earns less than 2 console repairs. Be selective when your reputation is high enough to skip small jobs.
  4. Leaving the shop without checking daily deliveries. Sometimes junk boxes contain rare, fully functional microchips worth 800-1,500¥.
