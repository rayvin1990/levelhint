ReStory: Chill Electronics Repairs Beginner's Guide — How to Start Your Repair Shop

Updated: August 2026 • 8 min read
Quick Answer:
  • Core loop: Buy broken devices → Place on work mat → Disassemble → Clean parts → Order replacements from web → Reassemble → Sell for profit
  • Your tools: Repair mat (for disassembly), shop computer (for web/parts), counter (for customer interactions and sales)
  • Key tip: Start with simpler devices (cell phones, music players) before tackling complex game consoles
  • Story matters: Your dialogue choices with customers affect their lives and change the ending you get

2. How the Repair System Works

The repair process in ReStory follows a consistent step-by-step flow for every device:

Step 1: Acquire a Broken Device

There are three ways to get devices to repair:

  • Customer drop-offs: Characters bring in their broken gadgets during story interactions
  • Online orders: Use the shop computer to browse retro marketplace websites and buy broken devices
  • Auctions: The Marketplace - Auction website lets you bid on devices — potentially cheaper but prices vary

Step 2: Disassemble the Device

Drag the device to your work mat and click it. The game switches to a close-up view where you can:

  • Remove the outer casing — unscrew panels, pop off covers
  • Extract internal components — circuit boards, screens, buttons, wiring
  • Inspect each part — broken parts are visibly damaged (cracks, corrosion, loose connections)

Step 3: Clean Dirty Components

Before replacing anything, try cleaning. Many devices just need a good scrub. Gameplay footage shows players using cleaning tools to remove dust, grime, and oxidation from components. A clean but functional part doesn't need replacing — saving you money.

Step 4: Order Replacement Parts

For components that are truly broken (not just dirty), open the computer and navigate to the Parts website. Browse available components, compare prices, and order what you need. Delivery takes in-game time, so plan ahead for multi-device repair sessions.

Step 5: Reassemble and Sell

Once all parts are cleaned or replaced, reassemble the device in reverse order. A fully repaired device can be dragged to the counter for sale. You'll see your profit — which is the sale price minus what you spent on parts.

4. Making Money in ReStory

Your income comes from the difference between a device's selling price and what you spent to fix it. Here's how to maximize profit:

  • Clean first, replace second. Don't buy a new part if the old one just needs cleaning. This is the single biggest money-saving habit.
  • Buy broken devices cheap on auctions. The Auction website often has better prices than direct purchase. Be patient and bid wisely.
  • Prioritize game consoles. Atari 2600, Nony PlayMachine, and Patento BS have the highest resale value once repaired — but only attempt them when you're comfortable with the repair system.
  • Complete customer story requests. Some customers offer premium prices for specific repairs tied to their personal stories.
  • Track your expenses. Parts cost money. If you over-order or replace components unnecessarily, you'll cut into your margins.

6. Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

  • Buying too many devices at once. You can only work on one at a time on the mat. Accumulating 10 broken devices just clutters your desk.
  • Skipping the cleaning step. Always inspect every component after disassembly. Many "broken" parts just need cleaning, not replacing.
  • Ignoring the auction website. The Marketplace - Auction often lists rare devices and parts below market price. Check it daily.
  • Rushing through story dialogue. Your conversation choices have permanent consequences on characters' lives and your ending. Read carefully.
  • Forgetting to sell repaired devices. Repaired devices sitting on your shelf aren't earning money. Drag them to the counter to complete the sale.
