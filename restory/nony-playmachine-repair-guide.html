ReStory: Nony PlayMachine Repair Guide — CD Drive, Disc Errors & Profit

Updated: August 2026 • 5 min read
Quick Answer — Nony PlayMachine at a glance:
  • Difficulty: Medium — a solid step up from cell phones.
  • Internals: CD drive mechanism, controller ports, main board.
  • Most common fault: Disc read errors (dirty laser lens or worn drive motor).
  • Profit: Mid-tier console ticket — worth taking, but below the Atari 2600.
  • Real-world inspiration: Sony PlayStation (parody brand "Nony").

What Makes the Nony PlayMachine Different

The Nony PlayMachine is ReStory's parody of the original Sony PlayStation. Unlike the cartridge-based Atari 2600, this console uses a CD drive mechanism — which introduces a whole new class of faults: disc read errors. It's the ideal "middle difficulty" device that bridges the gap between simple phones and the flagship Atari 2600.

Full Teardown — Step by Step

  1. Remove the outer shell. The PlayMachine has a clamshell design — top and bottom halves separate cleanly.
  2. Locate the CD drive assembly. It's the largest component — a motor, laser lens, and spindle.
  3. Clean the laser lens. Use a dry cotton swab (never alcohol directly on the lens — it can cloud the plastic). Gently wipe in a circular motion.
  4. Inspect the drive motor. A worn or seized motor causes discs to spin erratically. Listen for grinding during the diagnostic.
  5. Check controller ports. Same as the 2600 — look for cracked solder joints.
  6. Reassemble and test. Load a disc and confirm it reads before delivery.
Lens cleaning rule: Dry swab first, always. If the disc still won't read after a gentle clean, the problem is the motor or the laser itself — not dirt on the lens.

Disc error triage: If cleaning the lens doesn't fix the read error, test with a second known-good disc. If a second disc also fails, it's the motor or laser — a parts replacement, not a cleaning job.
