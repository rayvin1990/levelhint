ReStory Device Repair Tips — How to Fix Every Gadget Type (Phones, Consoles, Cameras & More)

Updated: August 2026 • 6 min read
Quick Answer — Common fault by device type:
  • Phones (Pokia, flip phones): Most issues are battery corrosion or screen ribbon cable. Clean before replacing.
  • Game consoles (Atari, retro handhelds): Dust-clogged cartridge slots and oxidized motherboard contacts. Use compressed air + alcohol.
  • Cameras: Stuck shutter mechanisms and dirty lenses. Don't force mechanical parts — lubricate with precision oil first.
  • Music players (PMP): Dead headphone jacks and failed capacitors. The solder joints on older players crack over time.
  • Audio receivers: Dust in potentiometers (volume knobs) and blown fuses. Clean the knobs with contact cleaner, check fuses first.

Universal Repair Rules (Apply to Every Device)

  1. Clean before replacing. At least 30% of devices brought in as "broken" are just dirty. Dust buildup, oxidized connectors, and gunked-up contacts look like component failure but aren't. Before spending yen on new parts, open the device and clean everything.
  2. Count your screws. The screw counter in the bottom-left of the repair interface is your best friend. Every device has a specific screw count per layer. Missing one at reassembly = device fails the final diagnostic = you disassemble again = lost time and profit.
  3. Diagnostic scan first, order parts second. Never order replacement components before running the full diagnostic on a device. The fault might be simpler than it looks.
  4. Same-day completion bonus. Customers pay more when you return their device on the same day they brought it in. Prioritize repairs you can finish before closing.

Phone Repair Tips (Pokia, Flip Phones, Smartphones)

Phones are your bread-and-butter early game, but they become low-value filler later. The most common phone issues:

FaultHow to FixTools Needed
Won't power onCheck battery contacts for corrosion first. Use alcohol + cotton swab. If contacts are clean, test battery voltage — it may need replacement.Multimeter, alcohol, tweezers
Cracked screenRemove bezel screws, lift the frame, disconnect the ribbon cable carefully. Ribbon cables on flip phones are fragile — use plastic opening tool, not metal.Precision screwdriver, plastic opening tool
No sound / speaker deadCheck the speaker grille for dust blockage. If grille is clean, the speaker module itself has failed. It's a replace part, not repairable.Compressed air, replacement speaker module
Stuck buttonsRemove keypad, clean membrane contacts with alcohol. Debris under the membrane is the culprit 90% of the time.Alcohol, cotton swab
Phone repair speed tip: Pokia phones (the early-game Nokia reference device) have only 4-6 screws and a single board. You can clear a Pokia repair in under 2 minutes with practice. Use these as quick filler between bigger console repairs.

Game Console Repair Tips (Atari, Retro Handhelds)

Consoles are your highest-paying repair tickets and the most rewarding. The repair difficulty scales up, but so does the payout.

FaultHow to FixTools Needed
No video output / black screenOpen the console. Check the power supply board capacitors — bulging or leaking caps are the #1 cause. Desolder the bad capacitor, solder in the replacement. Then test the video output chip with the multimeter.Soldering station, multimeter, replacement capacitors
Cartridge won't readClean the cartridge slot pins with alcohol + cotton swab or a cartridge cleaning tool. Don't use metal — you'll scratch the pin contacts. Blow compressed air into the slot first to clear dust.Alcohol, compressed air, cotton swabs
Overheating / shuts off randomlyCheck thermal paste on the main processor. If it's dried or cracked, clean the old paste with alcohol, apply fresh thermal compound.Alcohol, thermal paste, plastic scraper
Controller port not respondingThe solder joints on the controller port connector may have cracked. Reflow the solder on the connector pins. Check the port for bent pins — straighten with tweezers.Soldering station, tweezers
Console repair priority: If you have 2 consoles waiting and 1 phone, always do 1 console first (finish it), then the phone, then the second console. Console repairs often have 5-10 minute waits (thermal paste drying, soldering cool-down). Fill those wait windows with fast phone fixes.

Camera Repair Tips

Cameras have mechanical parts and tight tolerances. Don't force anything.

FaultHow to FixTools Needed
Lens won't extend / stuckSmall debris in the lens mechanism. Remove the lens ring, inspect the gear tracks. Clean with alcohol, apply a tiny drop of precision oil on the gear teeth — not on the lens glass.Precision screwdriver, alcohol, precision oil
Blurry / foggy photosLens is dirty or internal condensation. Remove the outer lens cover, clean the glass with a microfiber cloth. If fog is internal, leave the camera open in a dry spot for a full in-game day to evaporate.Microfiber cloth, lens cleaner
Flash doesn't fireCapacitor failure on the flash circuit. This is a replace-only fix — the flash capacitor module needs to be swapped out.Replacement flash capacitor
Shutter stuck closedMechanical seizure. Don't pry the shutter blades. Apply precision oil to the shutter hinge mechanism with a needle applicator, let it sit for 30 seconds, then gently actuate.Precision oil, needle applicator, tweezers

Music Player / PMP Repair Tips

Portable music players are the most nostalgia-heavy devices in the game. Repairs are straightforward but the older ones have specific failure patterns.

FaultHow to FixTools Needed
No sound outputCheck the headphone jack solder joints. Older PMPs develop cracked solder from repeated plug/unplug cycles. Reflow the solder on the jack connector. Also test with a known-good pair of headphones — sometimes the customer's headphones are the problem, not the player.Soldering station, multimeter
Screen flickering / dead pixelsRibbon cable connection to the display has loosened. Open the shell, reseat the ribbon cable connector. If flicker persists, the display module itself is failing — replace it.Plastic opening tool, replacement screen module
Battery dies quicklyStandard lithium battery degradation. Replace the battery module — it's one of the simplest repairs.Replacement battery module
Buttons unresponsiveSame as phones — clean membrane contacts with alcohol. Older PMPs collect skin oil and dust under the button pads.Alcohol, cotton swab

Audio Receiver / Amplifier Repair Tips

Audio gear is bulky but highly profitable. The most common issues are surprisingly simple.

FaultHow to FixTools Needed
Scratchy / crackling soundDust inside the potentiometers (volume knobs, tone controls). Spray contact cleaner into the potentiometer housing through the small opening, rotate the knob fully 10-15 times to distribute. This fixes ~70% of "scratchy audio" tickets.Contact cleaner spray
No power, no lightsCheck the fuse first — it's the simplest fix and often overlooked. If the fuse is intact, check the power supply board for bulging capacitors.Multimeter, replacement fuse
One channel silent (left or right)Trace the signal path from input to speaker output. The problem is usually a dead capacitor or transistor on that channel's amplifier board. Test components individually with the multimeter.Multimeter, soldering station
Audio gear diagnostic shortcut: Always test with headphones plugged in first. If the headphone output sounds fine but the speakers don't, the problem is in the speaker output stage — not the amplifier circuit. This narrows your diagnostic from 45 minutes to 10.
